MTLE 4150

Spiral Growth on a Dislocation

A kinetic Monte Carlo model of a close-packed (111) crystal facet growing from its vapor. Atoms land, hop across the terraces, stick at steps or leave again, with rates set by how many neighbors they have. A dislocation threading the facet leaves a step that ends at its core. Attaching atoms make the step turn around the core and wind into a spiral, so the crystal can keep growing without ever having to start a new layer.

The gray substrate fills the right part of the facet; its face has a deep and a shallow V-shaped notch. Young–Dupré with bond counting: cos θ = 2w/φ − 1 for a substrate bond w, so w = φ/2 gives θ ≈ 90°. The new layer nucleates in the notch tips. It escapes a notch easily only if the notch mouth is wider than the critical diameter 2r*; from a narrower notch it stays trapped (until a rare fluctuation frees it). Nucleation in the notch tips takes about a minute at Δμ/kBT = 0.5, and much longer at 0.4.

Facet width

2Growth conditions

φ is the energy of one bond between neighboring atoms. Δμ is how far the vapor is from equilibrium with the crystal: 0 is equilibrium, negative evaporates the crystal. The flat facet turns rough near kBT/φ ≈ 0.85 (the roughening transition). Above it it grows without steps.

3Surface diffusion

How many times an adatom with no neighbors hops before it would evaporate. More hops means atoms travel farther to reach a step, so fewer islands nucleate on the terraces. Each hop or evaporation slows by e−nφ/kBT for an atom with n neighbors.

The dashed circle is the critical 2D nucleus at the current temperature and Δμ: islands smaller than it tend to dissolve, larger ones tend to grow. r* = γas/Δμ, with as = (√3/2)a² the area per atom and γ the step free energy per atom spacing: φ for the broken bonds, lowered by the entropy of thermal kinks, γ = φ − kBT ln[(1+e−φ/2kBT)/(1−e−φ/2kBT)]. γ falls to zero near kBT/φ ≈ 0.82, the roughening point.

What to look for

  1. The step starts straight, running from the core to the right edge. The rest of the facet is a gentle ramp (the color shifts smoothly around the core), because the screw displacement lifts the lattice by one layer per turn.
  2. Atoms attach all along the step, so every part of it advances at about the same speed. Close to the core that means a faster turn, and the step winds up.
  3. It settles into a spiral whose turns are evenly spaced and rotate steadily. Each turn adds one layer. The spacing is set by the smallest island that can survive at that Δμ: raise Δμ and the spiral tightens.
  4. Without a dislocation (None, plus a disk), a flat facet can only grow by nucleating islands. A starting disk bigger than the dashed critical circle spreads to cover the facet, then growth stops: the new layer is complete and nothing nucleates. A disk smaller than r* dissolves. Change Δμ or the temperature and watch r* move past the disk.
  5. The notched substrate (None, plus a notched substrate) is nucleation in a crevice, in 2D. With a contact angle near 90°, a nucleus in the sharp tip of a notch is curved enough to be stable while still tiny, so the new layer starts there and fills the notch. At the mouth it bulges out as a half-circle the width of the mouth. If the mouth is wider than 2r* (the dashed circle), the layer escapes and spreads over the facet; if narrower, it stays trapped in the notch. On the open terrace or along the flat face nothing nucleates.
  6. A pair of opposite sign makes closed loops that spread out, as long as the two cores are farther apart than that critical island size; bring them closer and the source shuts off. A pair of the same sign makes two interleaved spirals.

Model: solid-on-solid crystal on a triangular lattice (each column of atoms has six neighbors), rejection-free kinetic Monte Carlo. Adsorption rate ν eΔμ/kBT − 3φ/kBT per site, so Δμ = 0 balances evaporation from a kink site, which has three of its six neighbors. Time is in units of 1/ν.
