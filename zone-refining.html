Zone Refining

Pfann's zone refining of a germanium ingot. An induction heater ring melts a narrow zone that you move along the bar. An impurity that dissolves better in the liquid than in the solid (distribution coefficient k = Csolid/Cliquid < 1) is left behind in the moving zone, so each zone sweeps impurities toward the far end. Pass after pass, the starting end becomes ultra-pure.

1Impurity

Smaller k: the impurity prefers the liquid more strongly and is swept away faster. Most impurities in germanium have k well below 1 (about 0.09 for gallium, 0.003 for antimony), which is what made zone-refined germanium so pure. Changes start a new ingot.

2Molten zone

Each trip of the heater ring along the ingot is one pass; when it reaches the end, press New pass to send the next ring. A longer zone removes more on the first pass but less in the end: the ultimate purity is better with short zones. Changing the zone length starts a new ingot.

The ring is drawn moving along a fixed ingot. In practice the ingot is pulled through fixed heaters, so the zone and the impurities travel the opposite way to the ingot.

3Plot

What to look for

  1. The first solid to freeze behind a zone has only k times the zone's concentration, so the starting end comes out purer (C = kC0 at the very start).
  2. As the zone moves, it collects the rejected impurity, and the solid it leaves behind gets richer. After one pass, Cs/C0 = 1 − (1 − k) e−kx/l, with x the distance from the starting end and l the zone length.
  3. The last zone length freezes like a normal casting and holds most of the impurity. Cut it off.
  4. Repeated passes keep pushing impurity to the far end, but each pass also brings some back: the profile approaches a limit, C = A eBx with k = Bl/(eBl − 1).

Model: the liquid in each zone is fully mixed, there is no diffusion in the solid, and the bar is 800 cells long. The ultimate curve is Pfann's analytical approximation; it treats the whole bar as exponential and ignores the last zone, so the simulated bar ends up somewhat purer than the curve at the starting end.
