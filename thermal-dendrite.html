MTLE 4150

Thermal Dendrite Growth

A pure melt solidifying in 2D. The solid on the left starts at the melting point; the liquid on the right is undercooled, and its far wall is kept cold, so the latent heat released at the front has to flow out through the liquid. The front starts flat, but a flat front pushing into colder liquid is unstable: bumps grow into fingers, and the crystal's anisotropy turns them into dendrites. Phase-field model of Kobayashi (1993), in its dimensionless units: the melting point is T = 1 and the latent heat is K.

1Cooling

Dimensionless undercooling Δ = (1 − T0)/K: how far below the melting point the liquid is, compared with the warming that its own latent heat can cause. Larger Δ, faster growth and finer structure. T0 moves the cold wall at once; the liquid starts at the new T0 when you start over.

2Crystal anisotropy

Symmetry

The interface energy is ε(θ) = ε̄[1 + δ cos j(θ − θ0)]. Dendrites grow along the directions where it is highest. With δ = 0 the fingers wander and split instead (seaweed).

3Noise and size

Grid

Noise seeds the first bumps on the flat front and the side branches later. With none, the front stays flat for a long time.

What to look for

  1. The flat front advances while the latent heat it releases piles up just ahead of it (the warm band in the liquid).
  2. Any bump that sticks out reaches colder liquid and sheds its heat in more directions, so it grows faster than its neighbors: the front is unstable (Mullins–Sekerka). Surface tension smooths very short bumps, so a definite spacing appears.
  3. The fingers compete. The leaders warm the liquid around them and hold back their neighbors, so the spacing coarsens. Leading tips sharpen into dendrites and grow side branches from the noise.
  4. Tilt the crystal axis: the dendrites grow along the crystal's preferred direction, not straight down the temperature gradient. Switch to 6-fold to see branches at 60°.

Model: τ ∂φ/∂t = ∇·(ε²∇φ) + ∂y(εε′∂xφ) − ∂x(εε′∂yφ) + φ(1 − φ)(φ − ½ + m(T) + noise), with m = (0.9/π) arctan[10(1 − T)], and ∂T/∂t = ∇²T + K ∂φ/∂t. φ = 1 is solid, 0 is liquid. Parameters as in Kobayashi (1993): τ = 0.0003, ε̄ = 0.01, grid spacing 0.03, time step 0.0002. Left wall insulated, right wall held at T0, periodic top and bottom.
